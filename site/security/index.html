How Amanfold keeps your vault private

This page explains, without jargon, what protects your vault and what does not yet. The full technical review, with every known open issue, is published next to the code.

Everything is locked on your device

When you save a password or a card, Amanfold encrypts it on your phone or computer before anything is sent. Each item has its own key, and those keys are locked by a key for the vault, which is locked by your account key. Only your devices can unlock that chain. Our server stores and passes along the locked result.

Even the names of your items and the websites they belong to are encrypted, and every item is padded to a standard size so its length does not give hints.

What our server stores about you

Can read

  • Your email address
  • Which vaults you share, and with whom
  • How much encrypted data you store
  • When your devices last synced

Cannot read

  • Your master password or Secret Key
  • Passwords, usernames and website addresses
  • Card numbers, expiry dates and security codes
  • Item names, notes and sign-in codes

Your master password and your Secret Key

Most password managers protect your vault with your master password alone. If their servers are breached, attackers can try billions of guesses against a copy of your vault. Amanfold adds a second secret: a random 128-bit Secret Key, created on your device when you sign up and printed in your recovery kit.

Your vault can only be unlocked with both. The Secret Key is never sent to us, so a stolen copy of our database cannot be cracked by guessing passwords, however weak yours is. Your master password is also stretched with Argon2id, which makes every single guess slow and memory-hungry.

Signing in without sending your password

Amanfold signs you in with OPAQUE, an internet standard (RFC 9807). Your device and our server do a mathematical exchange that proves you know the password, without the password, or anything that would let us or an attacker test guesses, ever reaching us. A fake server cannot learn it either.

A server that cannot cheat

We design as if our own server could be hostile. Every change to a vault is listed in a manifest signed by the account key of whoever made it. Your devices check those signatures and refuse anything that was swapped, removed, rolled back to an older version or moved from another vault.

Autofill that does not fall for fakes

Fake sites that copy a real one are the most common way passwords are stolen. Amanfold fills a password only on the exact site it was saved for, never fills on an insecure (http) page what was saved on a secure one, and warns you when a site looks like a copy of one you use, such as a 1 in place of an l.

Sharing with safety numbers

When you share a vault, its key is locked to the other person's public key and signed by you. Before a shared vault opens, you both see a short safety number. Compare it in person or on a call. If it matches, nobody, including us, has slipped their own key in between.

When someone leaves a shared vault, the vault gets a new key and every item is locked again, so they cannot read anything added after they left.

Getting back in

Because we never hold your keys, we cannot reset your password. Instead, you get a recovery kit when you sign up: your Secret Key and 24 recovery words. With the kit and a code sent to your email, you can set a new master password. Keep it somewhere safe, like with your passport. Trusted contacts for families and teams are planned.

Why open source does not make it easier to hack

Amanfold uses standard, publicly studied encryption: XChaCha20-Poly1305, X25519, Ed25519, Argon2id and OPAQUE. Its safety rests on secrets only you hold, not on keeping the design hidden. This is how the encryption in banking and messaging apps works too.

Publishing the code means anyone can check that we do what we say, and independent researchers can find mistakes so they are fixed in public. Release builds are made from the published code, and the apps pin our server's key so a look-alike server is refused.

What has and has not been checked

Amanfold is in public beta. Its design and code have been through our own internal review, which is published in full with every open issue. No independent firm has audited it yet. We will pay for an outside audit from our first revenue, publish the report, and remove the beta label only once every finding is fixed.

Known limits today include: the server, not the encryption, stops read-only members of a shared vault from writing; and a device that is already infected with malware can see what you unlock on it, as with any password manager.
